The plot flies your rocket through a step-by-step simulation: average thrust shaped to the motor's real curve, mass dropping as propellant burns, drag on the body tube, then the canopy's drag area once the ejection charge fires at burnout plus the delay.

Wind rises with height on a one-seventh power law, and the rocket weathercocks — it turns partway into the relative wind, which is why a breezy day lands it upwind of where you'd guess.

The circle comes from {{ cloudN }} runs with the wind, delay, weight, drag and canopy all jittered. Nine in ten of those land inside it. It is a good guess, not a promise: thermals, a tangled shroud line and a hard-packed field all beat the model.
